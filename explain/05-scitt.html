SCITT: statements, receipts, transparency

An issuer signs a statement, a transparency service records it in an append-only log and hands back a receipt, and anyone holding the two public keys can check both offline.

1What it is

SCITT is an IETF architecture in which an issuer signs a statement and registers it with a transparency service that keeps an append-only log.

On registration the service returns a receipt, a signed proof that the statement sits at a given position in its log at a given size.

A verifier holding the issuer's public key and the log's public key can check the statement and the receipt from the bytes alone, without contacting either party.

2Design choices

Chosen: a receipt is an inclusion proof in the service's log; beyond that it shows only the time of registration, and only when it carries a signed time claim.
Rejected: treating registration as the service agreeing with the statement; a receipt proves inclusion, not agreement. (RFC 9943; restated in draft-mih-scitt-checkpointed-local-log-02 §1.2, §4.5)
Chosen: the statement names its issuer and subject in signed CWT claims (iss, sub).
Rejected: registering any bare COSE_Sign1; a Producer Envelope without protected iss/sub is not a Signed Statement and a registrar wraps the capsule ID in one. (RFC 9943; draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 §3.3)
Chosen: the receipt names its verifiable data structure (vds, label 395) and carries the proof in its header (label 396).
Rejected: baking one log format into the statement; the same statement can carry receipts from logs built differently, and only the receipt changes. (RFC 9942)
Chosen: the receipt's payload is the tree root, detached, and the verifier recomputes it from the leaf and the path.
Rejected: other bytes in the payload slot; a signature over them binds nothing to the leaf whose inclusion the receipt proves, and the reference verifier rejects it. (RFC 9942; scitt-cose vector fail-attached-payload)
Chosen: each service publishes a Registration Policy, and independence comes from registering the same statement with more than one service.
Rejected: one service as the single source of truth; its view cannot be compared with anyone else's. (RFC 9943 §5.1.1 and §6.3, as cited in draft-mih-scitt-checkpointed-local-log-02 §1.2, §4.5)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • The issuer's key signed exactly these statement bytes (signature check).
  • The statement's leaf is at leaf_index in the log at tree_size, under a root the log key signed (inclusion).
  • The time of registration, but only if the receipt carries a signed time claim.
  • Given two signed tree heads and a consistency proof, that the log only appended between them.

Does not show

  • That the statement's content is true, or that the issuer was entitled to make it.
  • That the log is complete: anything the issuer never registered is simply absent.
  • That other parties see the same log; one receipt cannot rule out a different view shown elsewhere unless tree heads are compared through consistency checks.
  • That the issuer's key belongs to who you think; key authorization is the verifier's own policy.

5Specs

  • RFC RFC 9943 — SCITT architecture: issuers, signed statements, transparency services, Registration Policy.
  • RFC RFC 9942 — COSE Receipts: the vds and proof header parameters.
  • RFC RFC 9052 — COSE: the COSE_Sign1 structure used by both statement and receipt.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — how a capsule ID is registered as a Signed Statement (§3.3).
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-checkpointed-local-log-02 — registering a local log's checkpoints instead of every entry (next page).
  • Code action-state-group/scitt-cose — receipt verifier (Python, Go, Rust) and the vectors above.

Bilateral attestation and settlement

Two parties who do not trust each other each seal only what their own side observed, and a verifier who trusts neither derives from the two records whether they agree.

1What it is

Bilateral attestation is an exchange in which the requesting party signs what it asked for, the performing party signs what it did about it (performed, declined, or escalated), and each acknowledges the other's record.

Settlement records apply the same idea to a payment: the payer and the payee each seal, under their own key, only what their own system observed, as separate Agent Action Capsule leg records that cite one terms leg.

No record states whether the two sides agree; a verifier derives that from which records are present and whether their bound fields match.

2Design choices

Chosen: each sealer records its own side only, under its own key, and may cite the counterparty's leg as custody, never as its own observation.
Rejected: one party recording both sides; that is one party's account, the my-log-versus-your-log problem again. (settlement-records §3)
Chosen: the settlement state is derived by the verifier from the legs present; no member of a leg states it.
Rejected: a status field in the record; it would let one sealer state a fact only both sides' records can establish. (settlement-records §4.2, §9)
Chosen: exact amounts (integer string plus decimal scale) and the rule payer.amount = payee.received + payee.receive_fee, with no tolerance.
Rejected: comparing sent with received directly, or allowing a tolerance; an honest receive fee would read as a mismatch, and a tolerance would hide a real one. (settlement-records §6, §9.2)
Chosen: a verifier reports three distinct bilateral states: both halves present and agreeing, one half unavailable, both present and disagreeing.
Rejected: one "incomplete" outcome; a half that aged out of retention is not a finding against the record, while a disagreement is a contradiction that must stay one. (bilateral-attestation §10.1)
Chosen: a declined request is a completed exchange, recorded with its constraint basis and acknowledged by the refused party.
Rejected: treating a decline as a failed exchange; and a failure to countersign (delivery_unconfirmed, counterparty_timeout, countersign_refused) is kept apart from a decline. (bilateral-attestation §6, §8)
Chosen: signed objects from existing payment protocols are carried by digest, optionally with their exact bytes, and never re-signed.
Rejected: re-signing another issuer's object with the leg's own key, which a verifier reports as wrapped_resigned; restating its claims as the leg's own members is also forbidden, though no vector checks for it. (settlement-records §8, rule 3)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • That two records sealed under two distinct keys commit to the same payment reference, amount (after the receive fee) and status: agreed.
  • Which of amount, status and payment_ref differ, when both sides are present and joinable.
  • When only one side sealed: payer_stated or payee_stated, labelled as a stated claim.
  • When a payer-observed and a payee-observed leg share one key: sealer_conflation, never agreed.
  • Delivery separately: none, stated, matched or mismatch on the delivered content digest.
  • For a bilateral exchange: that a refusal was made and acknowledged, recorded with the same fidelity as performance.

Does not show

  • That either side's observation is true; agreement on a digest attests to the digest, not to what it represents (bilateral-attestation §10.1).
  • That a key belongs to the payer or the payee; that is the verifier's own key policy.
  • That a missing leg means disagreement; it may not exist yet, may not have been shared, or may never be produced.
  • Whether a fee was acceptable; agreed is about the payment only, and the terms comparison is reported separately as terms_amount.
  • That delivered content matches a description; with only a description_digest that judgment is outside the draft.
  • Who is right in a disagreement; neither half is preferred, and no dispute mechanism is defined.

A note on counts: the six slots above are the payment states of the settlement-records draft (§9.2). The bilateral-attestation draft defines three verifier states (§10.1), plus the three asymmetry dispositions and the orphan half of §8, and the reduced-assurance indicator of §7.

5Specs

  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-bilateral-attestation-02 — the four-move exchange, refusals, asymmetry dispositions, the three verifier states.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-settlement-records-00 — the four legs, exact amounts, payment references, derived payment and delivery states.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — the record each leg is, its capsule_id and Producer Envelope.
  • RFC RFC 8785 — JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS), the canonical form under each capsule_id.
  • RFC RFC 9052 — COSE; each leg's Producer Envelope is a COSE_Sign1.
  • RFC RFC 9943 — SCITT architecture; anchoring makes the record checkable by everyone else.
  • Code action-state-group/agent-action-capsule — settlement conformance vectors and their generator.
  • Code action-state-group/capsule-emit — capsule_emit.bilateral, the four-move handshake helpers.

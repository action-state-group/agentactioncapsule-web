Evidence grades

A record may say how much evidence stands behind it; a verifier works that out again from the record's own bytes and the proofs it can check, and reports any claim they do not support.

1What it is

The evidence layer names three levels of assurance a record can reach, self-attested (signed only by its producer, with neither a witness nor a countersignature), witnessed and countersigned, but defines no rule for a verifier to re-derive them.

The Agent Action Capsule draft does define such a rule for its own assurance fields: a verifier re-derives each one from what it can check, for cross_party_rung from the record's own cross_party block, and reports the derived value.

When a record claims more than that, the verifier reports the claim as an overclaim beside the derived value, and no draft turns any of this into a reputation number.

2Design choices

Chosen: a verifier re-derives each assurance mode from the evidence present and reports any overclaim.
Rejected: accepting the producer's label; a producer MUST NOT record a mode it did not achieve, and the verifier does not take its word for it. (AAC -05 §5.4, §6 check 7)
Chosen: how much of the other side's signed evidence is bound in is its own axis, cross_party_rung.
Rejected: folding a countersigned value into attestation_mode, which would make "both sides signed, not yet anchored" impossible to express. (AAC -05 §5.4.1)
Chosen: an overclaimed custody or cross-party grade is an informational finding, and the derived value replaces the claim.
Rejected: treating every overclaim alike; an unsupported time_rung: "witnessed" on a backfilled record fails verification, because the claim is unsupported and "falsifiable from the record's own bytes". (AAC -05 §6 checks 7 and 9)
Chosen: a record's epistemic_type (how its content came to be known) is fixed at commit and never upgraded.
Rejected: letting a later signature, receipt or countersignature change what kind of statement a record was. (Evidence layer -00 §4.1)
Chosen: report per record what the evidence supports, and leave weighing it to the relying party.
Rejected: a score for parties; the settlement draft "does not rank, rate, or otherwise grade the parties", and the bilateral draft "neither scores counterparties nor defines a vocabulary for doing so". (Settlement -00 §1.1; bilateral -02 §12.2)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • For each assurance field, the value the verifier can support: chained from an intact chain block, anchored only after it verifies an inclusion proof against a trusted log key.
  • For cross_party_rung, what the record's own cross_party block contains: an initiator_ref digest, a counterparty_ref digest and correlator, and a substantive flag.
  • Where a record claimed more than that, as a named finding with the claimed and the derived value.

Does not show

  • That the record's content is true; a grade says who signed and how it was committed, not how its content was known.
  • More than inclusion, from a Receipt: it shows a statement is in a log. Checking that each checkpoint extends the one before is a witness's job, a separate party (evidence layer -00 §3).
  • That a witness or countersigner is independent of the producer; that is a claim about who runs it.
  • That the other side signed anything: cross_party_rung is derived from the block's own bytes, without opening the digests it cites or checking the counterparty's signature; substantive: true is the producer's word.
  • Anything about a party's other records. There is no reputation number anywhere in these drafts.

4Example

A test vector for exactly this case: a record claims full_bilateral (both parties' evidence bound in) but its cross_party block holds only initiator_ref, a digest of the initiator's own signed half, and no counterparty_ref. The input, then the verifier's expected result.

FieldTypeMeaning
assurance.cross_party_rungtstrThe producer's claim: full_bilateral, both sides' evidence bound in.
cross_party.initiator_refhex tstrDigest of the initiator's own signed half; the only half present.
cross_party.counterparty_ref(absent)No other-side half is referenced, so the claim cannot hold.
derived.cross_party_rungtstrWhat the verifier derives: unilateral_fallback.
findings[].codetstrassurance_overclaim: claimed above derived.

5Specs

  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00 — defines self-attested, witness and countersignature, and fixes epistemic_type at commit.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — the assurance modes, cross_party_rung, time_rung, and the verifier's re-derivation.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-bilateral-attestation-02 — the two signed halves a cross-party grade summarizes; degraded records are always marked.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-settlement-records-00 — records both sides of a payment; does not rank or rate the parties.
  • RFC RFC 9943 — SCITT architecture: Transparency Service, Receipt, Auditor.
  • RFC RFC 9942 — COSE Receipts.

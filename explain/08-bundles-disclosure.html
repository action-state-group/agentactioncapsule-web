Bundles and selective disclosure

A bundle packages a record with what makes it checkable and says exactly what it left out; selective disclosure withholds single fields without breaking the signature over the record.

1What it is

An Evidence Bundle is one JSON object that carries a root record, the records it cites, any revealed preimages, and log evidence, without changing any record inside it.

It states its completeness as three separate claims, graph closure, interval coverage and per-record membership, and a verifier reports each one on its own.

Selective disclosure lets a producer replace single eligible fields with salted digests before signing, so a holder can later reveal some fields to a verifier and keep others back while the signature still verifies.

2Design choices

Chosen: citation closure is declared: every citation reached within closure_depth is either supplied or listed once in completeness.missing.
Rejected: assuming closure from what is present; a short chain would look complete. A verifier reports the depth it applied. (disclosure-bundle §4)
Chosen: three completeness claims, each verified and reported independently.
Rejected: one "complete" flag; an endpoint-only range proof still passes when an interior record is deleted or replaced, so per-record membership needs its own proof. (disclosure-bundle §6)
Chosen: without a verified checkpoint signature or witness receipt, coverage and membership are labelled checkpoint_unverified.
Rejected: reporting them as independently verified; they are then only relative to a checkpoint the producer asserted. (disclosure-bundle §6)
Chosen: a bundle-level disclosure overlay, keyed by capsule_id; a member not revealed is WITHHELD, never blank and never failed.
Rejected: wrapping each record with its disclosures; the overlay leaves each record and its capsule_id untouched. (disclosure-bundle §5)
Chosen: salted per-field digests with decoys, and capsule_id computed over the concealed form, so one signature serves every view.
Rejected: recomputing capsule_id over the revealed form; identity would then depend on which fields a verifier was shown, not on the event. (sel-disc §5.2, §8.4)
Chosen: the JSON form of the selective-disclosure model (SD-JWT, RFC 9901), hashing the JCS form, since the record payload is JSON.
Rejected: using SD-CWT directly; it is the CBOR/dCBOR sibling, cited for alignment, and hashes dCBOR rather than JCS. (sel-disc §3, Appendix)
Chosen: a closed list of eligible fields (operator, developer, some disposition and effect members, constraint records, framework_tags).
Rejected: concealing anything; hiding effect.status or a response digest would make base checks impossible, so that is a structural failure. (sel-disc §4.4, §8.6)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • That every citation reached within the stated depth is supplied with a matching identity, or openly declared missing.
  • That each supplied record sits at its claimed log position, by its own inclusion proof to the range root.
  • That a revealed preimage hashes to the digest the record committed to (REVEALED-match), or does not (REVEALED-mismatch).
  • That a revealed selective disclosure matches a digest under the signature; a mismatch is reported as sd_commitment_mismatch.
  • How many constraint records are still concealed, so a reader knows the visible list may be incomplete.

Does not show

  • Anything about records beyond closure_depth.
  • Interval coverage or membership as independent results while the checkpoint is unverified.
  • What a withheld field contains, or how many fields are concealed (decoys blur the count).
  • A full verification when a REQUIRED field such as operator stays concealed: ok is false, with sd_required_field_not_disclosed (sel-disc §8.5).
  • Anything from the verification member, which is producer self-report.
  • A valid countersignature: the slot is reserved and every entry is surfaced as unverified in this revision.

Two digest checks appear on this page. The bundle overlay compares the plain JSON digest of a revealed preimage (for example agent_input) with the digest the record carries. Selective disclosure compares the digest of a salted [salt, name, value] array with an entry in a signed _sd array.

5Specs

  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-zhang-agent-disclosure-bundle-00 — the Evidence Bundle: closure, overlay, the three completeness claims, bundle digest.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-sel-disc-00 — per-field salted commitments, decoys, eligible fields, verifier checks.
  • Internet-Draft draft-ietf-spice-sd-cwt-08 — SD-CWT, the CBOR sibling of the same selective-disclosure model.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — the record, its capsule_id and Producer Envelope.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-checkpointed-local-log-02 — related reading on checkpointed logs and MMR proofs; the bundle draft defines its own log-evidence shape and cites no revision of it.
  • RFC RFC 8785 — JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS), hashed by both digest checks.
  • RFC RFC 9052 — COSE; the Producer Envelope is a COSE_Sign1.
  • Code action-state-group/agent-action-capsule — Evidence Bundle vectors, and selective_disclosure.py in the Python package.

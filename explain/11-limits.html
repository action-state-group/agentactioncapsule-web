Limits

A verified key tells you who signed, not that what they signed is true; a twin and a referee test whether two answers agree under weights both nodes claim, which narrows a misstated model without establishing which model ran; hardware attestation is the direction for that.

1What it is

A signature that verifies shows that the holder of a key signed a record, and nothing about whether that key may speak for anyone or whether what it signed is true.

A node that misstates which model it ran signs that claim as easily as a true one; a twin and a referee can test whether two answers agree under weights both nodes claim, which narrows a misstated model but does not establish which model ran.

Hardware attestation, where the machine itself reports what it loaded and ran, is the route beyond self-report, and it is a direction here rather than a released feature.

2Design choices

Chosen: verification returns the authenticated key; whether it is authorized for the record's operator or developer is the caller's policy.
Rejected: building a key-to-organization binding into the wire format. (AAC -05 §3.2; the kid is a "self-attested signer identifier", §3.1)
Chosen: state plainly that controlling a key is not standing to act for anyone, and that identity is the floor of a record's weight.
Rejected: solving organizational identity inside these drafts; they inherit it. (Evidence layer -00 §11; bilateral -02 §12.1)
Chosen: a referee only for pairs a client marked, at temperature 0, same model and weights, answers that differ; exact words, no similarity threshold.
Rejected: guessing pairs from timing, or scoring answers by similarity. ("pairs are never guessed from timing": Mesh-LLM/capsules docs/TWIN-REFEREE-SELECTION.md at v0.1.4)
Chosen: the record keeps a hardware-attestation slot and writes it empty when no quote was taken.
Rejected: presenting a node's hash of its own binary as hardware evidence; at v0.1.4 the slot is "recorded absent, never fabricated". (Mesh-LLM/capsules producer/capsule.rs at v0.1.4; a producer MUST NOT record an assurance it did not achieve, AAC -05 §5.4)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • A verified signature proves the holder of key K signed this record's id.
  • Whether two twins gave the same words to the same request at temperature 0, under weights both their signed records claim are the same.
  • A referee's signed verdict records which twin its own re-answer agreed with at the earliest differing word, and names the other as contradicted.
  • The verdict seals the pair it rules on: both halves, the request digest, the weights digest and the referee's own answer.

Does not show

  • Who holds key K, or that K may speak for the operator the record names.
  • That what was signed is true, including the model name in the record.
  • Which model ran. The twins' matching weights are each node's own claim, and twins serving the same wrong model corroborate each other.
  • Which answer is right. A verdict is a ruling on one pair; it says nothing about a node's future answers.
  • That the referee is independent of either twin; that is a claim about who runs it.
  • Honesty across different hardware: exact replay can differ on different silicon for reasons unrelated to honesty, so such a mismatch is grounds to investigate, not a verdict.
  • What ran inside the machine. At v0.1.4 no hardware quote is taken.

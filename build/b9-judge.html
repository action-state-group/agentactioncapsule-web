Judging: a separate step from evidence

Seam ③ Check/judge. Build: judgment records kept apart from evidence records. Conform to: draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00 §4.1, §5.

Evidence says what was done, by whom, and that the record has not changed since. A judgment says whether it was right. Keep the two as separate records: a verifier can recompute what the evidence supports from its bytes, while the judgment stays labelled as one, naming who made it and on what basis.

What must hold
  • Verify before you show.
  • A judgment is never rounded into evidence.
  • History is counts anyone can recompute, never a score.

1What it is

Judging is the step after recording: something decides whether an act was right, and that decision becomes a record of its own, linked to the act by digest instead of written into it.

The judge depends on the work: rules a program can re-run, a judge model pinned to its model, prompt and sampling settings, with blind human ratings beside it, or, on a mesh, twins and a referee that signs a ruling on one pair.

A verifier recomputes what the evidence itself supports, reporting any assurance a record claims above that, and treats a judgment as what it is: a signed statement by a named judge, which it can check for integrity but not for correctness.

2Design choices

Chosen: a judgment is a new record carrying an adjudicates link to the act's digest; the act is never touched.
Rejected: writing a verdict field into the act record after the fact. “A link is itself part of a committed record and MUST NOT mutate its target.” (draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00 §5 Typed Links)
Chosen: the kind of statement a record is (epistemic_type) is set once, at commit, and never upgraded; a record with an adjudicates link MUST be of type adjudication.
Rejected: letting a later signature or checkpoint turn a judgment into something that reads like an observation. “epistemic_type is assigned once, at commit, and is never upgraded.” (draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00 §4.1, §5)
Chosen: the verifier recomputes each assurance value from the bytes and reports any claim above it. An overclaimed effect_mode fails verification; an overclaimed attestation_mode, ledger_mode or cross_party_rung is reported as a finding next to the derived value.
Rejected: trusting the record's own assurance block. “A producer MUST NOT record an assurance mode it did not achieve; a verifier rederives each mode from the evidence present and reports any overclaim.” (draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 §5.4, §5.4.1, §6; verify.py check 7)
Chosen: two answers kept apart on every claim: whether enough evidence existed to judge (SATISFIED / GAP / INSUFFICIENT / UNKNOWN), and only then the judgment (met / not_met / not_evaluable).
Rejected: one pass/fail figure, and using not_evaluable for missing evidence. “Every claim carries two answers that are never merged.” (agent-action-capsule spec/evidence-result-v0.md §1, design specification)
Chosen: say how a result was produced: recomputed (a fixed function of cited inputs, no model or human) or judged (a model or a human applying judgment, pinned).
Rejected: presenting a model's reading with the same standing as a recomputation anyone can repeat. (spec/evidence-plan-ir-v0.md §4.2)
Chosen: a judge model is pinned in the record: model_id, prompt_digest, optionally axes_digest, and sampling settings as integers, strings or booleans; each case cites the acts it rests on and the rubric (method), which is required.
Rejected: an unpinned judge, sampling settings as floats that can drift in serialization, and a confidence figure: that field has no counterpart in the family. (spec/judge-record-family-v1.md §2, §6, §9)
Chosen: humans rate cases blind (blind is fixed true), and a judge's calibration agreement is stored per clause as k of n.
Rejected: a blind: false rating in the same shape, and a stored rate: a reader divides k by n when reading. (spec/judge-record-family-v1.md §5, §8; vectors neg-blind-false, neg-clause-with-rate-field)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • Which assurance a record's own bytes support, and any claim above it, with the claimed and derived values side by side.
  • That a judgment exists, who signed it, what it cites by digest, and that it has not changed since it was signed.
  • What kind of statement it is (semantic_judgment, adjudication), fixed when it was committed.
  • For a pinned judge model: which model, prompt and sampling settings produced it, and which rubric each case used.
  • For a calibration summary: the judge's agreement per clause, as k of n.
  • For a referee's verdict: which twin its re-answer did not agree with, under the referee's signature.

Does not show

  • That the judgment is correct. A verifier can recompute a recomputed result; a judged one stays the judge's statement.
  • That the judge is independent of the party judged; that is a claim about who runs it.
  • That an act was right because its record verifies. The record shows what was done, not whether it was the right thing.
  • Anything about cases the judge did not see, or that a sample was not chosen to flatter.
  • A single figure for the agent. Judgments stay per case, and agreement stays k of n.

6Specs

  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00 — §4.1, the closed eight-value epistemic_type set, including derived_metric, semantic_judgment and adjudication, never upgraded; §5, the adjudicates link, which never mutates its target.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — §5.4 Assurance and §5.4.1 Cross-party assurance, where the verifier recomputes and reports overclaims; §6, Class 1 verification.
  • RFC RFC 9052 — COSE: the COSE_Sign1 signature on the act and on the judgment.
  • RFC RFC 8785 — JSON Canonicalization Scheme: the bytes every digest a judgment cites covers.
  • Design spec judge-record-family-v1 — the shapes of a judgment record; not an Internet-Draft.
  • Code action-state-group/agent-action-capsule — the verifiers (Python, Go, TypeScript) and the overclaim vectors.
  • Code Mesh-LLM/capsules v0.1.4 — the referee and its signed verdict record.

7What you need before the next step

  • A verifier that recomputes every assurance value from the record's bytes and reports any claim above it, instead of reading the claim.
  • A judgment record of its own: a fixed epistemic_type, an adjudicates link (or citations) to what it judges by digest, and the judge's basis (a rule, a pinned model plus rubric, or a referee's re-answer).
  • A way to tell recomputed results from judged ones, blind human ratings of a judge model's cases, and its calibration agreement kept as k of n.
  • “Was there enough evidence?” and “was it right?” as two answers, never one, so the report in the next step can show both.

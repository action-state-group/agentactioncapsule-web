Agent work for someone else

Seam ② Contract. Build: both sides' records of a deal between a principal's agent and a counterparty. Conform to: draft-mih-agent-evidence-request-00 and draft-mih-agent-settlement-records-00.

When an agent acts for a principal and deals with a counterparty, three parties hold records, and the evidence layer has to let each one check the others without trusting them.

What must hold
  • Verify before you show.
  • A judgment is never rounded into evidence.
  • History is counts anyone can recompute, never a score.
Status: next — designed, not built as a whole. No host on this site's path yet records a principal, an agent and a counterparty end to end. Each side's sealed half of a paid exchange has been sealed on two live nodes in the Mesh-LLM worked example. The parts that exist in code at a pinned commit are the settlement-record legs, the evidence request protocol, and one composition vector set joining a WHAT record to a WHO record; section 5 lists each with its evidence.

1What it is

Agent work for someone else is an exchange with three parties: a principal who authorizes the work, an agent that does it, and a counterparty the agent deals with, often with a payment between them.

Each party seals only what its own side observed, under its own key, and asks the others for evidence with a request whose answer is exactly one of three things: the artifact, a signed refusal with a reason, or the asker's own record that nothing came.

Separate records answer separate questions (was it permitted, who authorized it, what was done, did the runtime enforce), and they are joined by one digest of the action rather than merged into one format.

2Design choices

Chosen: each party seals its own side only, under its own key, and may cite the other side's record as something it holds, never as its own observation.
Rejected: the agent, or the platform it runs on, recording both sides; that is one party's account of a two-party event. (settlement-records §3; bilateral-attestation-02)
Chosen: whether a payment's two sides agree is derived by a verifier from the legs present.
Rejected: a leg that asserts the settlement state (agreed, mismatch); one sealer would then state a fact only both sides' records can establish. (settlement-records §1 item 7, §9)
Chosen: an evidence request ends in exactly one of three outcomes, and none is ever recorded as another: a timeout is never written up as a refusal, and a signed refusal is never written up as silence.
Rejected: treating silence, a transport error and a stale cache as one "failed" result; they carry different weight, and only a refusal is the responder's signed act. (evidence-request §4.5)
Chosen: the same subject under the same coverage anchor yields byte-identical evidence for every requester; a responder may refuse someone, but may not answer two askers differently.
Rejected: per-audience answers, a fuller history for an auditor and a curated one for a counterparty in a dispute (the draft's "flattering version"). (evidence-request §1.1, §5)
Chosen: the ask is symmetric: either party to an exchange may ask the other, naming its own sealed half as the subject.
Rejected: a one-way audit channel where only the original requester asks; the side holding a signed reply is the one that can show the other side's log left something out. (evidence-request §3.1, §6)
Chosen: CAN, WHO, WHAT and AUDIT records join on a shared digest of the action, or an explicit signed cross-reference (§4); digest equality is a join key and nothing more.
Rejected: growing one format to contain the others, or letting one slot's verifier vouch for another slot's content. (accountability-composition-01 §4, §5, §6.3.4)
Chosen (Mesh-LLM worked example): the provider seals its payee_observed leg only from what its own wallet reported crediting and deducting.
Rejected: filling the payee leg from the invoice amount; the invoice is not what the wallet credited, and a missing fee never reconciles. On a host that does not pass those numbers, no payee leg is sealed. (Mesh-LLM/capsules crates/evidence-plugin/src/settlement_legs.rs)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • That two records sealed under two distinct keys report the same payment reference, amount (after the receive fee) and status, or which of those differ.
  • That a responder answered, refused with a stated reason under its signature, or that the asker recorded no answer inside its waiting window.
  • That a responder gave two askers different bytes for the same subject and anchor: they compare artifact digests, without trusting each other (not for exchange subjects, which only one asker holds; there the responder's own recorded ask is the second copy, evidence-request §5).
  • An omission: a party holding the other side's signed reply citing its half, when that side now answers no_such_subject (evidence-request §3.1, §6).
  • That a WHO record and a WHAT record name the same action, by recomputing the digest each one binds.

Does not show

  • That either side's observation is true; agreement on a digest attests to the digest, not to what it stands for.
  • Who holds a key in the real world; that is the verifier's own key policy.
  • Why nothing came back: a partition, a crash and a deliberate silence look the same (evidence-request §4.4).
  • That evidence stays available; a responder's signed retention commitment makes a later absence attributable, not impossible (evidence-request §9).
  • That the action was authorized, or that the record set is complete, just because digests match (accountability-composition-01 §6.2).
  • Who is right when the two sides disagree; neither record is preferred.

6Specs

  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-evidence-request-00 — the request, the three outcomes, caller invariance, the symmetric ask, retention commitments.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-settlement-records-00 — two independent sealers, the four legs, exact amounts, derived payment and delivery states.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-sato-agent-accountability-composition-01 — CAN, WHO, WHAT and AUDIT composed by a shared action digest; assurance tiers; cross-slot conformance.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-bilateral-attestation-02 — each side's attestation of its part of an exchange, refusals across the boundary, and the three asymmetry dispositions (delivery_unconfirmed, counterparty_timeout, countersign_refused).
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — the record every half and every leg is.
  • RFC RFC 8785 — JSON Canonicalization Scheme: the JSON bytes under the evidence request digest and under the action digest in the WHAT×WHO vector set.
  • RFC RFC 8949 — CBOR: the evidence request's CBOR binding, in deterministic encoding.
  • RFC RFC 9943 — SCITT architecture: registering a record so a party who trusts neither side can check it.
  • Code action-state-group/agent-action-capsule — the drafts' vectors, the evidence-request crate, settlement libraries.
  • Code action-state-group/capsule-emit — settlement producer and verifier; bilateral handshake helpers.
  • Code Mesh-LLM/capsules — the worked example: settlement legs and evidence requests in a Mesh-LLM plugin.

7What you need before the next step

  • A host event for each paid exchange that names both sides and carries what each wallet reported, so each side can seal its own legs.
  • A way for two parties to push sealed records to each other, and a channel to carry evidence requests and their answers.
  • A deterministic artifact for each (subject, anchor), so two askers can compare what they were given.
  • A place for the principal's authorization to live as its own signed record, joined to the agent's record by the action digest, rather than inside it.
  • An honest status per part: which records your host seals today, and which are designed only.

Conform

Seams ① Record to ⑤ View. Build: nothing new; check that what you built is read the same way by code you did not write. Conform to: the conformance vectors published beside each Internet-Draft.

An evidence layer is portable when a stranger's verifier accepts your bundle without talking to you, your host, an account or an operator. Four checks get you there: run the vectors, pass a verifier you did not write, keep your own fields in the slot made for them, and hand the bundle to someone else.

What must hold
  • Verify before you show.
  • A judgment is never rounded into evidence.
  • History is counts anyone can recompute, never a score.

1Run the vectors

Each draft ships frozen bytes and the result a verifier must reach on them. Run them in your tests, unchanged, pinned to a tag. A conforming implementation accepts every positive case and rejects every negative case for the stated reason; passing only the positive ones shows nothing.

# a known-good conformance vector  ->  ok: True (one info finding: chain checks need a store)
agent-action-capsule verify vectors/capsule/pos-v4-jcs-chain-committed/input.json

# a tampered capsule               ->  ok: False, capsule_id_mismatch
agent-action-capsule verify vectors/capsule/neg-v4-chain-tampered/input.json

From the agent-action-capsule README at db9de0f0d2b78efdd096dc4016a63785cca96601. Every directory under vectors/capsule/ is one case: input.json is the capsule, expected.json the verifier's expected result.

python3 commitment-conformance-vectors/reference_verifier.py
python3 mmr-profile-vectors/generate.py --check
cd python && python3 -m pytest tests/checkpoint

From the checkpointed-local-log README at 32ce9f3f901ea85c80a4b42b6fd3ee007fdda2c0: the log's checkpoint and proof vectors.

Vector sets for the other drafts, the Evidence Bundle, the Evidence Request and the Evidence Result among them, are in agent-action-capsule/vectors.

2Pass a verifier you did not write

Write your bundle in the open format, evidence-bundle/v2, not a schema of your own, and check it with a verifier that was written independently of your producer.

capsulectl verify --bundle BUNDLE.json [--witness-directory WITNESSES.json]

From the capsule-cli README, Commands at f8aefe98445eaa9217e24627e255681c3857ae3d.

It says VALID (exit 0) when the checkpoint's signature, the interval coverage, each record's place in the log, the citation closure, the records' signatures and every disclosed payload all check out; INVALID (exit 1) when a record or payload changed after sealing; INCOMPLETE (exit 3) naming what the bundle does not show. VALID means the signatures verify under the keys the bundle carries. Whether you trust those keys is a separate step:

jq -r '.records[].key_id, .checkpoint.key_id' bundle.json | sort -u   # here: "$public"

From the same README, worked example: compare the keys it prints with the producer's public key, obtained some other way.

3Keep your own fields in the slot made for them

A format stays checkable by strangers only if everyone keeps to its closed shape. Where a draft gives you room for your own data, use that room and nothing else. In an Evidence Result, the document and each claim admit members whose name begins with x- (lowercase); any other member that the specification does not define fails validation (Evidence Result v0, §8a). A verifier never needs to understand your x- members to check the rest.

A kind of record or field that other implementations should read belongs in the registry, not in a private name: the interim registry of record is spec/REGISTRY.md until IANA registries are established.

4What portable means

Hand your bundle to someone who has never talked to your host. With only the file and your public key, obtained from you some other way, their verifier reaches the same verdict yours does. If it needs your service, your account or a call back to you, the layer is not portable yet.

  • No default service in the path: a witness only when you name one.
  • The vectors in your tests, unchanged and pinned.
  • At least one verifier you did not write, run in your CI against a bundle your code produced.

Independent and same-team implementations, and the evidence for each, are listed in the specification repository's INTEROP.md and on Specifications.

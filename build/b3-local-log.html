The local evidence log

Seam ① Record. Build: an append-only local log with signed checkpoints and inclusion proofs. Conform to: draft-mih-scitt-checkpointed-local-log-02 (reference implementation: checkpointed-local-log; any conforming log).

Once you can seal a record, keep every one you seal in an append-only log on your own disk, sign a small checkpoint over it every N records or T minutes, and hand out a record with its inclusion proof and checkpoint so anyone can check it offline.

What must hold
  • Verify before you show.
  • A judgment is never rounded into evidence.
  • History is counts recomputed from sealed records, never a score.

1What it is

A local evidence log is a producer's own append-only log of the records it seals: each record's id goes in as a leaf of a Merkle Mountain Range (MMR) on the producer's disk, and nothing leaves unless the producer sends it.

Every N records or T minutes the producer signs a small checkpoint over the whole log. Any record can then be shown to sit at a position under that checkpoint (an inclusion proof), and any later checkpoint can be shown to extend it (a consistency proof).

What you export is one record plus its inclusion proof plus the checkpoint: a file or a push that another party checks offline against the producer's key. The producer can also register its checkpoints with a transparency service it does not run.

The reference implementation is checkpointed-local-log (CLL), in Python, Go, TypeScript and Rust. Any log that meets the CLL draft's checks is acceptable: a verifier checks the proofs and the signed checkpoint, not which library produced them.

Where the library sits today

The Mesh-LLM plugin is a worked example with four layers, each in its own repository. They are listed bottom-up, with the versions pinned in the plugin's v0.1.4 Cargo.lock and the current release of each.

2Design choices

Chosen: the log holds a 32-byte digest per record (the plugin uses capsule_id), and its leaf value is SHA-256(0x00 ‖ entry).
Rejected: putting record content in the log. A digest lets the content be erased from separate storage later while its entry stays in the log, and the 0x00 prefix keeps a leaf from ever matching an interior node. (CLL §1.1 "Entry", §2 item 2, §3)
Chosen: append each record when it is sealed or received, and never modify or remove an entry.
Rejected: assembling the log at the end of a period, which "permits curation that no later verification can detect". (CLL §2 items 1 and 2)
Chosen: checkpoint on two triggers, a count and a clock. capsules v0.1.4 uses 100 records or 300 s, the clock running from when a tick first sees records no checkpoint covers. It also cuts a checkpoint at start-up, at shutdown, and when a record is about to be pushed (at most one per 100 ms).
Rejected: checkpointing only when someone asks. Once a producer publishes checkpoints, the draft requires it to declare a cadence and meet it, because "a checkpoint regime with discretionary gaps supports none of the claims". Registering every record as it is made is the central-log cost the draft is written to avoid. (CLL §4.4 item 1, §1; capsules checkpoint_cadence.rs)
Chosen: before a checkpoint leaves the node, pad the log with padding records until its record count is a multiple of a bucket size. capsules v0.1.4 defaults to 32; a setting of 0 turns padding off.
Rejected: exact sizes. Anyone holding two checkpoints would learn how many records the producer committed between them, across every counterparty, not only their own. (Evidence Layer -00 §12.1; capsules CAPSULES_CHECKPOINT_PAD_BUCKET)
Chosen: the book embeds its commitment log and exposes no type from it. Callers ask for records, bundles and answers, and the book builds every proof.
Rejected: a book tied to one mechanism. The Evidence Layer draft requires four properties (append order, inclusion, consistency, checkpoint identity) and no particular mechanism. A checkpointed local log is one way to meet them; registration with a SCITT service is another. (Evidence Layer -00 §8)
Chosen: checkpoints stay local by default. They go to a transparency service only if the operator names one (the plugin's setting calls it a witness: CAPSULES_CHECKPOINT_WITNESS_URLS), and an unreachable service never blocks local checkpointing.
Rejected: sending checkpoints out by default. Also rejected: counting a service the producer runs itself as independent, since the draft calls that "a replica". (capsules checkpoint_cadence.rs, CAPSULES_CHECKPOINT_WITNESS_URLS; CLL §4.4 item 3)

3What it shows / what it does not

Shows

  • The producer signed every checkpoint and chose what to present, so the next three points hold only as far as the verifier trusts the producer (CLL §5.3):
  • A record with a given capsule_id is in the log at a given position under a checkpoint the producer's key signed (an inclusion proof, as in the example below).
  • A later checkpoint extends an earlier one without rewriting it (a consistency proof, CLL §4.3 step 8).
  • A presented range of records is complete under one checkpoint (CLL §6).
  • Once checkpoints are registered under a Consistency Registration Policy at a service independent of the producer, each record existed no later than the first registered checkpoint covering it, and the registered history was not rewritten (CLL §7).

Does not show

  • That any record's content is true (CLL §7).
  • That the producer appended everything it did, or that this is its only log (CLL §7).
  • When a record was made, if no checkpoint was registered. The checkpoint's time is the producer's own claim.
  • Anything beyond the producer's own word for records appended after the last registered checkpoint (CLL §4.4 item 4).
  • The exact number of records: padding hides it only to within the bucket size, and checkpoint times still show when the producer is active (Evidence Layer -00 §12.1).
  • In v0.1.4, a gap in checkpoints as something a verifier can detect. The plugin cuts no checkpoint while nothing new is appended. The -02 draft asks a producer that publishes checkpoints to declare a cadence and keep to it even when idle (CLL §4.4 item 1).

Recompute it

  1. leaf 7 = SHA-256(0x00 ‖ d804c0bf…), over the 32 bytes of capsule_id.
  2. node 9 = SHA-256(be64(10) ‖ leaf 7 ‖ c93642d9…): the parent's position plus one, then left child, then right child.
  3. root = SHA-256(node 9 ‖ d1b17708…) = 6473683c…, the checkpoint's root. Then check the checkpoint's signature under key_id.

Under CLL -02 the same log at size 10 would carry commitment = node 6 ‖ node 9 (64 bytes, tallest peak first), and a bagged root "MUST NOT be carried" (CLL §4.1). The explain series' "Checkpointed local log and MMR" page walks through a -02 checkpoint.

  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-checkpointed-local-log-02 — the log discipline (§2), the leaf (§3), the checkpoint and its checks (§4, §4.3), required cadence (§4.4), registration (§4.5), and what it does and does not establish (§7).
  • Internet-Draft draft-bryce-cose-receipts-mmr-profile-03 — the MMR, its node hashing, and inclusion and consistency proofs.
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00 — what a book must supply (§8) and what a checkpoint reveals, including padding (§12.1).
  • Internet-Draft draft-mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule-05 — the record whose capsule_id is each entry.
  • RFC RFC 9943 — SCITT architecture: registering a checkpoint as a Signed Statement.
  • RFC RFC 9942 — COSE Receipts: what a transparency service returns for a registered checkpoint.
  • RFC RFC 8785 — JSON Canonicalization Scheme: the bytes capsule_id covers.

7What you need before the next step

  • A local log that records every exchange you take part in, as requester or provider, signed with a key the other side can check.
  • A checkpoint cadence you can state (N records or T seconds), and padding to a bucket size before any checkpoint leaves.
  • An export the other side can check offline: the record, its inclusion proof and the checkpoint.
  • A way to send your half to the other side and keep theirs. The next step compares two halves of one exchange and needs both.
